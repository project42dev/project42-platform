# Choose a Deployment Shape You Can Actually Operate: reduced-motion presentation

Present every visual as a complete static composition. Do not make completion
depend on animation timing, autoplay, or pointer gestures.

## workload-contract-narration

Display the complete form at once and mark the current field group with a persistent border and text heading.

Text alternative: The deployment decision begins with users, tasks, data, demand, latency, availability, failure behavior, and recovery rather than a hardware or cloud product.

## workload-demonstration

Show both contracts side by side with changed fields labeled in text; do not animate between them.

Text alternative: The experiment can stop safely and has six users; the production service affects hundreds and requires stronger availability, capacity, telemetry, ownership, and recovery.

## shape-comparison-narration

Keep all rows and columns visible. Use a text marker and border to identify the current cell without moving the matrix.

Text alternative: Every shape offers different control, capacity, connectivity, physical, operating, cost, and recovery tradeoffs; no row is automatically best.

## failure-domain-narration

Show all nested boundaries and dependency labels simultaneously. Use numbered descriptions for each failure test.

Text alternative: Replicas protect against some process failures but share a host and several external dependencies, so they do not prove host or site availability.

## ownership-narration

Show the complete matrix as a static table and identify missing owners with text, an icon, and a border rather than color alone.

Text alternative: Each model-service responsibility has a named accountable owner who can act, produce evidence, respond or escalate, and be replaced by a qualified substitute.

## reversible-choice-narration

Present the decision record as a numbered static sequence and keep every field visible.

Text alternative: The first deployment is selected to prove one claim safely and includes evidence requirements, stop conditions, a known-safe return, migration path, and review date.
