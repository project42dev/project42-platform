# Research with Evidence: reduced-motion presentation

Present every visual as a complete static composition. Do not make completion
depend on animation timing, autoplay, or pointer gestures.

## bounded-brief-explanation

Display all six fields simultaneously with numbered headings and no animated transitions.

Text alternative: The brief connects one decision to bounded questions, scope limits, evidence requirements, permitted data, and explicit stopping rules.

## brief-demonstration

Present a two-column Before and Bounded Brief comparison without morphing or moving text.

Text alternative: The revised brief names the task, language, test set, load, safety behavior, data constraints, regions, cost period, and stopping rules.

## source-ledger-explanation

Show the full table with written status labels; do not animate rows changing categories.

Text alternative: Each row identifies the source, date, supported claims, limitations, access date, and whether it is accepted, rejected, disputed, or still a lead.

## claim-verification-explanation

Display the completed map as a static table and use text labels rather than color alone.

Text alternative: Claims are labeled supported, disputed, inferred, or unsupported after checking source passage, date, version, region, and authority.

## recovery-explanation

Display all failure and response pairs in a numbered table rather than revealing branches over time.

Text alternative: Inaccessible, stale, circular, ambiguous, conflicting, and insufficient evidence each lead to a narrower search, test, gap report, or accountable escalation.

## removed-source-demonstration

Show before and after tables side by side with the changed support status written explicitly.

Text alternative: The claim map shows two supported claims and one newly unsupported latency claim after its test evidence is removed.

## safety-and-provenance-explanation

Show the complete checklist with persistent text and no animated completion effects.

Text alternative: The checklist verifies minimum permitted data, instruction boundaries, attribution, working citations, visible limitations, evidence scope, and reproducibility.
