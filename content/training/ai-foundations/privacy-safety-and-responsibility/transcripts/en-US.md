## Welcome: Welcome

Welcome. Responsible AI use begins before you type a prompt and continues after a result reaches a person. In this lesson, you will establish an input boundary, scale safeguards to consequence, examine privacy, security, fairness, transparency, and accountability, and create a practical responsible-use plan. We will work with the fictional Harbor Hands Community Network dossier, then compare your reasoning with a changed Lakeside Food Share case. This lesson uses synthetic instructional material. It does not establish legal compliance, privacy assurance, universal tokenizer measurements, product approval, vendor retention practices, or tested model behavior. Manual work is always valid when evidence, controls, product terms, or staffing are inadequate.

Sources:

- <https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework>

## Narration: Protect Inputs

Use a pause-before-paste rule. Identify what the information contains, whether you are authorized to use it, which product, account, and workspace are approved, what the product retains, who can access it, whether it may be used for improvement, and how deletion works. Sensitive information may include personal data, health or financial records, credentials, private source code, customer material, confidential plans, regulated data, or combinations of ordinary facts that identify a person. Minimize before prompting. Remove fields the task does not need, redact or substitute where possible, use approved products and accounts, and never enter secrets into prompts, examples, screenshots, or transcripts. Data minimization happens before prompting, not after the response is generated. If authorization, product processing, retention, access, deletion, or improvement-use terms are unknown, stop or use a manual process.

Stop. Removing names does not establish that the messages are anonymous, permitted, or protected. Combined facts may identify people. The team must confirm authorization, approved service and account, retention, access, training or improvement use, deletion, contractual terms, and the minimum content needed. A safer design may aggregate categories, use synthetic examples, run an approved controlled service, or avoid the upload. A disclaimer does not repair an unauthorized disclosure. If you chose to proceed because the task seems helpful, revise the rule: expected benefit never substitutes for permission and data governance.

Correct feedback: You recognized that name removal alone does not establish anonymity, authorization, or safe service handling.

## Narration: Risk

Risk follows consequence, exposure, and reversibility. A private brainstorming draft and an automated eligibility decision may use similar language technology but have radically different consequences. Ask who is affected, how widely the output spreads, whether errors can be corrected, and whether the system can take action. Increase evidence, testing, human review, access controls, monitoring, and recovery as consequences rise. Human review is not magic. A reviewer needs time, authority, relevant evidence, and a real ability to reject or correct an output. Some uses should remain prohibited when harm cannot be controlled to an acceptable level. A small audience does not make a high-consequence decision safe, and a disclaimer does not create reversibility.

Sources:

- <https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework>

## Narration: Dimensions

Responsible use has several dimensions. Privacy asks whether data is collected, used, retained, shared, exported, and deleted appropriately. Security addresses unauthorized access, prompt injection, data leakage, malicious tools, and system abuse. Fairness examines whether performance and impact differ unacceptably across people or situations. Transparency means people understand when AI is involved, what its role is, what important limitations apply, and how to question or correct an outcome. Accountability assigns owners who can investigate and respond. These dimensions are distinct. Encryption does not prove fairness. A fairness test does not prevent credential theft. A disclosure banner does not create a correction path. Translate each material concern into an owner, a preventive control, a detective control, and a recovery path.

## Narration: Build Controls Explanation

Build controls around the whole workflow. Start with purpose, owner, intended users, affected people, approved data, prohibited data, expected behavior, and excluded decisions. Map plausible misuse and failure. Define launch criteria and tests using representative cases, including difficult and adversarial examples. Prevention controls can minimize data, scope credentials, allowlist tools, filter inputs, validate formats, rate-limit use, and require approval before consequential actions. Detection controls can compare outputs to evidence, monitor quality and disparity metrics, record audit events, detect unusual access, and collect user reports. Response controls assign triage, notification, correction, containment, and escalation. Recovery controls restore records, roll back releases, revoke access, provide appeal, and retire an unsafe feature. Google describes policy, evaluation, and safeguards as layers; it also warns that classifiers can produce false positives and false negatives. Measure the safeguards themselves. Document residual risk and who accepted it. Monitor after launch because data, models, users, attacks, and operating conditions change. Set a re-evaluation trigger, a stop condition, and a retirement owner before the first real user is affected.

## Narration: Arithmetic

Now inspect the exact controls and arithmetic. Every proposed assignment requires coordinator approval before communication, publication, or use. The pre-action approval rate is numerator: proposed assignments with approval recorded before action, divided by denominator: all proposed assignments in the monitoring window, multiplied by one hundred. The threshold is one hundred percent. Unsupported-assignment rate is unsupported approved assignments divided by all eligible approved and reviewed assignments, multiplied by one hundred. The threshold is zero percent. Prohibited-input-event rate is prompts containing prohibited input divided by all prompts for this use case, multiplied by one hundred. The threshold is zero percent. Unreviewed-publication rate is assignments published without prior approval divided by all published assignments, multiplied by one hundred. The threshold is zero percent. Volunteer-correction rate is eligible communicated assignments receiving a substantiated permitted-fact correction divided by all eligible communicated assignments, multiplied by one hundred. The threshold is below five percent. The window is 2026-10-03 through 2026-10-31 inclusive. If any denominator is zero, the result is UNKNOWN, not zero percent. No observations or counts are asserted here. Approval is separate from a later quality audit. A quality audit cannot substitute for one-hundred-percent pre-action approval, and a small sample cannot establish absence of rare failures.

## Narration: Recovery

Before entry, redact the data and use an approved account and workspace with least-privilege access, fixed role and availability fields, and no messaging or record-editing tools. Before approval, compare every draft with the source roster, record the reviewer and decision, log the prompt version, and flag uncovered roles or uncertainty. If approval is missing, pause and prevent communication or use. If an unsupported assignment occurs, quarantine it, compare the prompt and roster, correct the assignment or roster as appropriate, document the decision, and choose continue, redesign, manual-only, or retirement. If prohibited data enters a prompt, stop prompting, preserve the incident record, identify exposure, revoke access when necessary, notify affected people when warranted, escalate under the fictional policy, and restore the manual process. If an assignment is published without approval, remove or quarantine it, preserve the audit record, correct or retract it, and notify recipients when warranted. If the volunteer-correction rate reaches five percent or more, pause and investigate. After every pause, document one response: continue with controls unchanged, redesign, operate manual-only, or retire. The plan is instructional content, not evidence of deployment or safety.

## Narration: Changed Case Context

Now decide independently. Lakeside Food Share proposes using AI to rank 240 volunteer applications for access to paid weekend shifts. One staffing manager would see the output, but applicants' income opportunities could be affected and there is no appeal route. Proposed inputs are names, addresses, availability, prior shift notes, and free-text comments. There is no tested performance evidence, no approved product processing terms, and no permission for automatic ranking. Before you choose, distinguish three decision options. Launch means proceed with the proposed workflow only after required evidence and authorization are confirmed. Assistance-only redesign means bounded drafting or support with authorized minimal input, while humans decide and the system does not autonomously allocate people or opportunities. Manual process means authorized humans handle the task under existing controls while AI evidence gaps remain. Manual work is not automatically safe, and assistance-only is not automatically approved. For any option, identify what data is necessary, who has authority, what evidence is missing, and how to stop or recover.

## Feedback: Key Feedback

The causal reasoning is: paid-shift ranking can affect income, so consequence is high; 240 applications create meaningful exposure even though one manager sees the output; no appeal route makes reversibility low; missing product terms, tested evidence, approved authority, and human recourse are launch blockers. Stop AI ranking, preserve manual processing, notify the manager that no AI output may determine access, and process applications manually. If an output exists, quarantine it, prohibit decision use, preserve an audit record, and provide correction or appeal before any future pilot. Future metrics need a numerator, actual eligible denominator, observation window, and threshold. Zero eligible cases are UNKNOWN. Small samples require limitations. Any unsupported recommendation, material disparity signal, unauthorized processing, missing approval, or failed appeal path pauses the pilot.

Correct feedback: Manual processing follows from high consequence, meaningful exposure, low reversibility, and unresolved approval, evidence, product-term, and recourse gates.

Retry feedback: Reconsider whether one manager reduces consequence, whether all free text is necessary, and whether nominal human review has authority, evidence, time, and recourse.
