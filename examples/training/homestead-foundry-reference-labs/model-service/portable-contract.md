# Portable model service lab contract

## Mission

Deploy and defend one bounded model service without coupling mastery evidence to a
particular provider, portal, or orchestrator.

## Inputs

- A bounded workload, intended users, prohibited uses, data classes, and
  service objectives.
- An immutable model and tokenizer identity with publisher, source, revision,
  digest, license decision, provenance, dependencies, and scan disposition.
- A versioned runtime, driver or execution provider, template or adapter,
  gateway policy, configuration, evaluation set, load profile, and recovery plan.

## Outputs

- A schema-valid endpoint response and explicit error, timeout, cancellation,
  rejection, and overload behavior.
- Evidence tied to the exact serving build for artifact identity, access,
  evaluation, load and capacity, telemetry, update, rollback, and recovery.
- A human release disposition, residual risks, operating owner, and next review.

## Required exercise

1. Verify the exact serving unit before admitting traffic.
2. Prove caller identity and authorization with positive and negative cases.
3. Run routine, important-slice, boundary, malformed, unauthorized, timeout,
   overload, dependency, and recovery cases.
4. Measure latency and first-output distributions, throughput, queue time,
   rejection, error, saturation, warm-up, and attributable cost.
5. Correlate secret-safe logs, metrics, traces, build identity, policy decisions,
   evaluation references, and terminal outcomes.
6. Qualify a candidate change and obtain authorized human approval.
7. Force a predeclared gate failure, stop exposure, reconcile in-flight work, and
   restore the complete known-good serving unit.
8. Verify identity, authorization, quality, capacity, telemetry, cost, and
   user-visible postconditions after recovery.

## Stop conditions

Stop before execution when artifact identity, license, authority, target, or data
boundary is unresolved. Stop during execution on a critical evaluation or access
failure, prohibited effect, protected-data disclosure, uncontrolled queue or
saturation, telemetry blindness, cost ceiling, or failed rollback.

## Evidence rule

The lab remains a draft while approvals are empty or evidence digests are
illustrative. A screenshot, portal status, health response, or model statement
cannot substitute for reproducible evidence and accountable human review.
